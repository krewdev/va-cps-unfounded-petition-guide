Virginia Guide: Unfounded CPS Reports & the § 63.2-1514 Petition Process

This page consolidates information about discovering the identity of a reporter and related remedies after an unfounded child protective services (CPS) investigation in Virginia, particularly in the context of false allegations during custody or criminal proceedings.

Reporter Confidentiality Is the Default

Under federal law (CAPTA) and Virginia Code, the identity of a person who reports suspected child abuse or neglect is protected. Reports may be made anonymously. Local departments of social services (DSS) will not simply disclose the name.

Upon request, DSS will tell you if an unfounded report was made anonymously, but will not disclose the identity itself (§ 63.2-1514(C)).

The § 63.2-1514 Petition Process

Virginia Code § 63.2-1514(D) provides a specific procedure for the subject of an unfounded report or completed family assessment who believes the report was made in bad faith or with malicious intent.

Eligibility

  • You are the subject of the report (the person alleged to have committed abuse or neglect).
  • The disposition is unfounded or a family assessment was completed.
  • You believe it was made in bad faith or with malicious intent.

Step-by-Step Process

  1. Confirm unfounded disposition and obtain the written letter from the local DSS. Note the jurisdiction where the report was made.
  2. Draft a petition that specifically sets forth the factual reasons you believe the report was made in bad faith or with malicious intent. Attach the disposition letter.
  3. File in the Circuit Court of the city or county where the report was made. Include Form CC-1416 (Cover Sheet for Filing Civil Actions). Pay fees or request a waiver.
  4. Upon filing, the court requests the DSS records for in camera (private) review.
  5. You are entitled to present evidence supporting your petition.
  6. The court determines whether there is a reasonable question of fact as to bad faith/malicious intent and that disclosure would not likely endanger the complainant’s life or safety.
  7. If both are met, the court provides you a copy of the investigation/family assessment records (which may include the reporter’s identity).
  8. The original records become subject to discovery in any subsequent civil action regarding the bad-faith report.

Local DSS is required to notify you of this procedure when informing you of an unfounded disposition.

Proving Bad Faith or Malicious Intent

For the petition, the threshold is a “reasonable question of fact.” For overcoming immunity (§ 63.2-1512) in a later civil suit, or for a criminal charge, the bar is higher.

From case law (including Wolf v. Fauquier County Bd. of Supervisors, 555 F.3d 311 (4th Cir. 2009)):

  • Malicious intent requires some form of malign motive.
  • Bad faith involves dishonesty of belief or purpose.
  • Negligence, mistake, or misunderstanding is not enough. An unfounded finding alone is insufficient.

Common types of circumstantial evidence include timing relative to custody litigation or a trial, prior pattern of allegations, evidence of coaching a child, unique knowledge in the report, and motive evidence.

Criminal Knowingly False Report (§ 63.2-1513) vs. the Petition

These are complementary, not alternatives.

  • Petition (§ 63.2-1514): Lower threshold (reasonable question of fact). Primary tool to obtain records and identity. Controlled by you.
  • Criminal (§ 63.2-1513): Requires proof the person knew the report was false. Class 1 misdemeanor (subsequent offense felony). Conviction forces purging of records. Decision rests with the prosecutor.

Many people start with the petition to gather information, then evaluate a criminal referral or civil claim.

Sample Petition Template

This is a starting template only. Customize every bracketed section with your specific facts. Have a Virginia attorney review before filing. Vague or conclusory reasons are insufficient.

VIRGINIA:
IN THE CIRCUIT COURT OF THE [CITY/COUNTY] OF [NAME]

[YOUR FULL LEGAL NAME],
     Petitioner,

v.

[LOCAL DEPARTMENT OF SOCIAL SERVICES, e.g., RICHMOND CITY DEPARTMENT OF SOCIAL SERVICES],
     Respondent.

CASE NO. _______________

PETITION FOR RELEASE OF RECORDS PURSUANT TO VIRGINIA CODE § 63.2-1514(D)
(Unfounded Report Alleged to Have Been Made in Bad Faith or with Malicious Intent)

COMES NOW the Petitioner, [Your Full Legal Name], pro se / by counsel, and respectfully petitions this Court as follows:

1. Petitioner is an adult resident of [City/County], Virginia, and is the subject of an unfounded report or complaint of child abuse or neglect made pursuant to Chapter 15 of Title 63.2 of the Code of Virginia.

2. On or about [approximate date of report], a report or complaint was made to the [Local] Department of Social Services alleging [brief, non-inflammatory description of the nature of the allegations, e.g., physical abuse, sexual abuse, neglect of a minor child].

3. After investigation [or family assessment], the local department determined the report/complaint to be unfounded [or completed a family assessment]. Petitioner received written notice of this disposition on or about [date]. A copy of the disposition letter is attached as Exhibit A.

4. Petitioner believes the report or complaint was made in bad faith or with malicious intent for the following specific reasons:
   a. [State concrete facts—e.g., the report was made shortly before a scheduled custody hearing in [case number] on [date];]
   b. [The suspected reporter has a documented history of prior unfounded allegations or interference with Petitioner’s relationship with the child(ren);]
   c. [The allegations contained details known only to [specific person];]
   d. [Any other specific, factual reasons supported by documents, statements, timelines, or other evidence.]
   (Attach supporting documents as additional exhibits if available.)

5. Petitioner has no history of violence or threats toward the complainant and states that disclosure of the complainant’s identity would not be likely to endanger the complainant’s life or safety.

6. Pursuant to Virginia Code § 63.2-1514(D), Petitioner requests that this Court:
   a. Direct the [Local] Department of Social Services to provide its records of the investigation or family assessment to the Court for in camera review;
   b. Allow Petitioner to present evidence in support of this petition;
   c. Determine that there is a reasonable question of fact as to whether the report or complaint was made in bad faith or with malicious intent and that disclosure of the complainant’s identity would not be likely to endanger the life or safety of the complainant; and
   d. Provide to Petitioner a copy of the records of the investigation or family assessment, including information identifying the complainant.

WHEREFORE, Petitioner respectfully requests that this Court grant the relief requested herein and such other and further relief as the Court deems just and proper.

Respectfully submitted,

[Your Full Legal Name]
[Your Address]
[Your Phone Number]
[Your Email]

_________________________________
[Signature]
[Date]

[If represented: Attorney name, VSB number, address, etc.]

Attachments:
Exhibit A – Unfounded Disposition Letter
[Additional exhibits as needed]

Practical Notes

  • Unfounded records are generally retained for three years (extendable).
  • Address the impact of false allegations in any ongoing family court custody case (best interests of the child).
  • Strongly consult a Virginia attorney familiar with DSS matters before filing.
  • Official source: Virginia Code § 63.2-1514.
